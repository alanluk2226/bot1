The computer is the brain. The ESP32 only moves the eight servos.

Loaded cell types

Click a cell type to highlight it. These 167 neurons only carry heading and left-right steering. Sugar, shadow, antenna, and mouthpart neurons are not loaded.

Behaviors on this page

Walk forward

When the compass error is within about 30°. The front-left and rear-right feet step, then the front-right and rear-left feet.

Turn

A larger error lengthens the outside step and shortens the inside step. DNa02 left and right rates are shown. The turn size follows the EPG angle.

Stand

After stop is typed in the terminal, all eight servos return to 90° and stay powered.

Hardware limits

  • Commands arrive over Wi-Fi on port 8765
  • No distance sensor and no camera

How these neurons reach the robot

EPG compassHeading sits in a bump on the ellipsoid body. The computer reads the population vector as an angle.
PEN / PEGCompass update circuit. When the head turns, the bump moves around the ring.
PFLConnects the compass to descending neurons. They stay in the steering circuit on this page.
DNa01 / DNa02One descending neuron on each side. A large error shortens one side's step and the body turns.

Not on this robot

The full fly brain is about 140,000 neurons, including vision, taste, the giant-fiber escape circuit, the proboscis, and motor neurons for six legs. Those are outside this set of 167, so this robot does not react to sugar, shadows, or antennae.
